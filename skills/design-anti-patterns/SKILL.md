# Design anti-patterns

A list of what **not** to do. It is a constraint on output, not design direction —
pair it with whatever skill is setting the direction, and apply it to anything a
client will see.

## Visual anti-patterns: avoid the "AI-generated website" look

Do NOT use these defaults unless the brief explicitly calls for them:

1. **Purple-blue gradients.** No violet-to-blue hero backgrounds or buttons. Pick a palette from the client's brand.
2. **Inter on everything.** Don't default to Inter (or any single generic sans) for all text. Choose type with character that fits the brand.
3. **Gradient text.** No multi-color gradient fills on headlines. Use solid color and let weight and size do the work.
4. **"Introducing" badges.** No small pill or eyebrow label above the hero headline ("INTRODUCING", "New: AI-powered…").
5. **Glowing halos.** No ambient glow, blurred color blobs, or radial light behind elements.
6. **Frosted glass.** No glassmorphism cards (translucent and blurred over a gradient).
7. **Icon tiles.** No rounded gradient squares holding an icon above each feature heading.
8. **Identical feature cards.** No 3×2 grid of same-size cards with an icon, title, and one line of copy. Vary layout and give content real hierarchy.
9. **Cards inside cards.** No nested bordered or rounded containers. One level of containment, max.
10. **Side-stripe cards.** No thick colored left border on cards or callouts unless it's an actual alert.
11. **Giant vanity stats.** No oversized "10M+ ACTIVE USERS / 99.9% uptime" blocks, especially with made-up numbers.
12. **01 / 02 / 03 labels.** No numbered section or step labels as decoration.
13. **Pulsing green dot.** No "Systems operational" status indicators unless the product really has a status page.
14. **Buzzword copy.** No "supercharge", "world-class", "enterprise-grade", "next-generation", "unlock", or "seamless". No em dashes in copy. Write plainly and specifically about what the client actually does.
15. **Beige + italic serif.** Don't swap to the "tasteful" fallback of a cream background with a serif headline that has one italicized word. It's just as recognizable.

**General rule:** if a choice is the first thing that comes to mind for a generic SaaS landing page, question it. Derive colors, type, layout, and copy from the specific client, their industry, and their audience.

## How to apply it

Check the list twice: once when planning the palette, type and layout, and again
on the finished page before the client sees it. Read the rendered result, not the
intent — several of these creep in while implementing something else.

Report each one found and what replaced it. Do not silently leave one in place.

## When the brief asks for one

The brief wins. If the client asks for glassmorphism, or their brand palette
genuinely is violet-to-blue, build it — these are defaults to avoid reaching for
unprompted, not banned techniques. Say which item the brief overrode, so the
choice is visibly deliberate rather than a default that slipped through.
